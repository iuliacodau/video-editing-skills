# Cinematography for vertical social video

Help the user capture footage that is easy to edit and looks intentional. Output is a **shot list** and practical shooting advice.

## Process

1. **Get context.** Script or brief, location, subject, gear (phone or camera, tripod, lights, mic), and time available.
2. **Plan coverage per beat.** For each script beat, choose a shot that makes the idea visible. Aim for a mix of shot sizes so the edit has variety (see `references/shot-types.md`).
3. **Add b-roll and inserts.** For every spoken claim, think of a cutaway: hands, objects, the environment, a screen recording. Capture more than needed.
4. **Set up the frame.** Vertical 9:16, subject placed with the platform UI in mind (keep key content clear of the top, bottom and right edges, which apps cover with buttons and text). Check current platform safe zones before finalizing.
5. **Light the subject.** Use a soft light source at roughly 45 degrees to the subject and slightly above eye level, with a window as the easiest option. Keep light direction and color consistent across shots in one scene. Avoid mixed color temperatures.
6. **Stabilize and move with purpose.** Prefer a tripod or locked-off phone. Use movement (push-in, pan, handheld) only when it supports the beat.
7. **Shooting checklist.** Lens clean, exposure and focus locked, 24 or 30 fps (match the project; 60 fps only for planned slow motion), clean audio recorded, a few seconds of room tone, and extra seconds at the start and end of each take for cutting.

## Output format

| Shot | Beat | Size and angle | Movement | Notes (lighting, props, audio) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Followed by a short **order of shooting** (grouped by location and lighting setup, not script order) and a **b-roll wishlist**.

## Principles

- Every shot should have a reason: what does the viewer see or feel here?
- Get the hook shot right first. It is the most important shot in the video.
- Shoot in the same lighting and settings within a scene so grading stays simple.
- Record clean audio. Poor audio hurts retention more than a plain image.
- Capture reaction shots, hands, details and establishing shots; they solve editing problems later.
