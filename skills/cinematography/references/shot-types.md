# Shot types and camera notes

## Shot sizes

| Size | Frames | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extreme close-up | One detail (eyes, texture) | Emphasis, tension, product detail |
| Close-up | Face or object fills frame | Emotion, direct address, key moments |
| Medium close-up | Chest up | Standard talking-head framing |
| Medium | Waist up | Conversation, demonstrations |
| Wide | Subject and surroundings | Establishing place, scale, context |

## Angles

- **Eye level:** neutral, relatable. Default for talking to camera.
- **High angle:** subject looks smaller or vulnerable; also useful for top-down process shots (hands, food, desk).
- **Low angle:** subject looks stronger or larger.
- **Dutch tilt:** use sparingly for unease or energy.

## Movement

- **Locked-off:** clean and stable, easiest to edit.
- **Push-in:** builds focus or tension.
- **Pull-out:** reveals context or ends a beat.
- **Pan / tilt:** follow action or reveal.
- **Handheld:** energy and immediacy; keep it deliberate, not shaky by accident.
- **Whip pan or match cut:** transitions that hide a cut.

## Composition

- Rule of thirds: place eyes near the upper third line.
- Headroom: small but not zero. Leave room for captions near the lower third.
- Leading lines and depth: foreground, subject, background.
- Background: tidy, with some separation from the subject (distance or light).
- Leave space where on-screen text will go; do not plan text over the busiest part of the frame.

## Talking-head setup

- Camera at eye level, lens near the eyes, subject looking at the lens.
- Light from the front-side through a window or soft source; avoid light directly behind the subject unless it is a deliberate rim or backlit look.
- Mic as close as practical. Record a test and listen with headphones.
- Re-record a flubbed line from the start of the sentence so it cuts cleanly.

## B-roll categories

- Hands doing the task
- Close details of objects
- Environment and establishing shots
- Screen recordings
- Reaction shots and looks off-camera
- Transitions: doors, passing objects, covered-lens moments

## Phone settings

- Use the main (not digital-zoomed) lens for best quality.
- Lock exposure and focus (tap and hold).
- Turn off auto-switching of lenses and unneeded beautification filters.
- Shoot at the highest resolution and a consistent frame rate for the whole project.
