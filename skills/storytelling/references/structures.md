# Story shapes and structures

Pick the shape that fits the true story, then size it to the platform. Do not force a shape the material does not support.

## Core shapes

**And / But / Therefore (any length).** Context, then conflict, then consequence. The minimum viable story and the best diagnostic. Chain several "but / therefore" pairs for longer videos.

**Pixar spine (20-60s).** Once upon a time there was [someone]. Every day [routine]. One day [disruption]. Because of that [effect]. Because of that [effect]. Until finally [resolution]. Good for mini-stories and anything with a clear cause-and-effect chain.

**Man in a Hole (15-60s).** Someone is doing fine, falls into trouble, climbs out, ends better than they started. The single most reusable shape: failures, fixes, projects, comebacks.

**Cinderella / rise, fall, bigger rise (30-90s).** Things improve, a setback hits, then a larger win. Good for journeys and transformations.

**Normal, Explosion, New Normal (10-45s).** From Kindra Hall: how things were, the event that disrupted them, how things are now. Strong for personal and brand stories. Add an identifiable person, a real feeling, one significant moment and specific details.

**Story Circle, compressed (45-90s).** You in a comfort zone; you need something; you go somewhere unfamiliar; you adapt; you find it; you pay a price; you return; you have changed. Use for longer, arc-driven pieces.

**Five-second moment (any length).** Start with the opposite of the realization, build stakes, and end on the moment of change. Cut anything that does not lead there.

## Formats mapped to shapes

| Format | Useful shape |
| --- | --- |
| How-to or tip | ABT with the creator's mistake as the "but" |
| Before and after | Man in a Hole, with the "before" as the hole |
| Myth versus reality | The belief is the "and", the evidence is the "but", the better method is the "therefore" |
| Listicle | ABT for the frame, with each item as a mini payoff, strongest last or first on purpose |
| Day in the life | Pixar spine, with one disruption as the "one day" |
| Skit or POV | One want, one obstacle, one twist ending |
| Loop (7-20s) | The final frame leads into the first |

## Sizing to length

- **7-15s:** one ABT sentence. Hook is the conflict or the result; the payoff is the last beat.
- **15-30s:** hook, setup, attempt, turn, payoff. One obstacle only.
- **30-60s:** add a second gap (a failed attempt before the working one) and a clear before and after.
- **60-90s:** add a stakes beat and a moment of doubt. Still one idea.

## Hook styles

Hooks work when they are made from the real story, not pasted on.

- **Conflict:** open on the problem or failure.
- **Result first:** show the end, then how it happened.
- **Surprise or violated expectation:** a fact or moment that contradicts what the viewer assumes.
- **Curiosity gap:** pose a question the video answers later.
- **In medias res:** start mid-action, then rewind.
- **Pattern call-out:** "If you [specific situation]..."

Checks: Works with sound off? First frame interesting? Specific to this video? Can the video deliver on it?

## Pacing guide (rough)

- Rule of thumb, not a researched figure: many editors aim for something new on screen every few seconds (a cut, zoom, new angle, graphic or caption emphasis), often roughly every 1-3 seconds in fast formats. Let the content set the pace, and check the platform's retention graph to see where viewers actually leave.
- Remove breaths, filler words and dead air before adding effects.
- Vary rhythm: fast runs of cuts, then a held shot for the key beat.
- Put the slowest, most important beat just before the turn so tension builds.
