# Worked examples

Both examples are **illustrative**. The user's answers are invented to show the process. In a real session, only use what the user actually says.

---

## Example 1: A flat sentence with no struggle

**Draft**

> Today I edited a 30-second reel for a bakery and it turned out great.

**Verdict**

There is a clear subject and a happy ending, but the draft is a report. We never learn what was hard, what was at risk, or what changed, so a viewer has no reason to stay for the outcome.

**Story check**

- Want: Thin (make a reel for a bakery, but nothing specific)
- Obstacle: Missing
- Stakes: Missing
- Gap: Missing
- Change: Missing
- Chain: Missing (single event)
- Specifics: Missing
- Loop and payoff: Thin ("turned out great" is a conclusion without a question)

**Biggest gap:** obstacle. Success without struggle.

**Questions (round 1)**

1. What was the difficult part of this edit?
2. Were there any constraints, such as the footage, the deadline, or what the client asked for?
3. What did you try first, and did it work?

**Invented answers**

> The owner sent 40 phone clips, all shaky and bluish. She wanted it to feel "cozy" and needed it by the next morning. My first try with a filter made the bread look fake.

**Reflect back**

So you had a night, shaky bluish footage, a client who wanted cozy, and a first fix that backfired. That is the "but". As a sentence: *A bakery owner sent me 40 shaky clips and wanted a cozy reel by morning, but my first filter made the bread look fake, so I had to find another way.*

**Questions (round 2: stakes and change)**

1. What was riding on this reel (a first client, a launch, a reputation)?
2. What did you finally do that worked, and what was the moment you knew?

**Invented answers**

> It was my first paid client and she was launching Saturday. Around midnight I stopped adding filters and just matched the white balance on one good clip, then copied it to the rest. The bread went golden instantly.

**Story in one sentence (And / But / Therefore)**

*I took my first paid edit, 40 shaky bluish clips and a "make it cozy" brief due at sunrise, BUT my first filter made the bread look fake, THEREFORE I dropped the filters and fixed one thing, white balance, and the golden warmth appeared on its own.*

**Beat map (30s, Pixar-style spine)**

1. **Hook (0-3s):** the fake-looking bread (the failure) with on-screen text.
2. **Setup (3-8s):** first paid client, 40 clips, deadline.
3. **Attempt and failure (8-15s):** filters, the bread looks plastic.
4. **Turn (15-23s):** midnight decision, one hero clip, matching white balance.
5. **Payoff (23-30s):** before and after, the golden bread, the client's reaction `[?]`.

**Hook options (from the real story)**

- Result first: the golden bread, then "I almost ruined it with a filter."
- Conflict: "My first paid client wanted cozy. I gave her plastic bread."
- Surprise: "The fix for cozy footage was removing things."

## Example 2: Informational content with no obvious conflict

**Draft**

> Here are three tips for color grading: fix exposure first, match your shots, and don't oversaturate.

**Verdict**

Useful, but it is a list. Nothing pulls the viewer forward, and nothing shows why these tips matter. The tension can come from the creator's own mistake or the viewer's common mistake.

**Biggest gap:** stakes and obstacle (why should the viewer care, and what goes wrong without the tips?).

**Questions**

1. Which of these three did you get wrong yourself, and what happened?
2. What does a beginner's edit look like when they skip these?
3. Is there a specific project where this mistake showed?

**Invented answer**

> I used to crank saturation to make footage "pop". A client said my skin tones looked sunburnt and I re-did a whole video.

**Reframe**

*I thought saturation was how you make footage pop, BUT a client said everyone looked sunburnt, THEREFORE I re-graded the whole video using three rules.* The three tips become the "therefore", not the whole video.

**Beat map**

1. Hook: the sunburnt before frame with on-screen text.
2. The mistake and the client's comment.
3. Rule 1, then proof on screen (before and after).
4. Rule 2, then proof.
5. Rule 3, then proof.
6. Payoff: same shot, fixed.
