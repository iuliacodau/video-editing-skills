# Question bank

Use these to interview the user. Pick **two or three** from the gap you are working on, rephrase them in the user's own words and content, and never fire the whole list.

Good questions are concrete, ask for a memory rather than an opinion, and are easy to answer in a sentence or two. If the user struggles, offer options.

## A. Finding the story (only an idea or topic is given)

- What happened recently that you would tell a friend about over coffee?
- What surprised you while doing this?
- If a viewer remembers one thing, what should it be?
- Who is this for, and what do they currently struggle with or believe?
- What did you get wrong at first?

## B. Want

- What exactly were you trying to achieve?
- Why did it matter to you, or to the person you did it for?
- What would success have looked like?
- Was there something underneath the goal, like proving something, avoiding embarrassment, or finally finishing it?

## C. Obstacle (the usual gap in flat drafts)

- What was the difficult part?
- What did you try first, and what happened?
- Were there constraints: time, budget, gear, skills, an unreasonable request, a deadline?
- What went wrong, or almost went wrong?
- Who or what pushed back, even a little?
- What was the easy way out, and why did you not take it?
- What did you not know at the start that you know now?

## D. Stakes

- What would have happened if it had failed?
- What was at risk: money, reputation, a relationship, time, pride?
- Was there a deadline, or someone waiting or watching?
- What did it cost you (sleep, other plans, confidence)?
- Why should a stranger care whether this works out?

## E. The gap (expectation versus result)

- What did you expect to happen?
- What actually happened?
- When did you realize the plan was not working?
- What was the most unexpected moment?
- What did you do next, and why?

## F. Change

- How did you think or feel at the start, and how at the end?
- What did you believe back then that turned out to be wrong?
- Was there a single moment when it clicked or shifted?
- What would you tell yourself at the start?
- What do you do differently now?

## G. Specifics and senses

- Take me to that exact moment: where were you, what time was it, what could you see or hear?
- What object, number, line or sound do you still remember?
- What did you say or do in that second?
- What would a camera have filmed?

## H. Emotion

- What did it feel like right then, and where in your body?
- What were you afraid of?
- Was there a moment of doubt, frustration or relief you can describe plainly?

## I. Ending and payoff

- How did it end, and what is the proof?
- What is different now?
- What do you want the viewer to feel or do afterwards?
- Does the end answer the question the video opened with?

## J. Viewer relevance

- What is the viewer's version of this struggle?
- What common belief does this challenge?
- Why would someone keep watching after the first three seconds?

## K. When the user is stuck

- **Worst moment:** What was the worst or funniest moment of the whole thing?
- **Polar opposite:** What were you best at here, and what threw you off?
- **What would not happen next:** List a few things that would not be expected, and see if any is true.
- **Say it out loud:** Tell it as a short voice note, then pull the best sentence out of it.
- **Different angle:** If the struggle was someone else's, what was theirs?

## Conversation style

- Start by naming what works, then the main gap.
- Ask, wait for the answer, then reflect back before asking more.
- Use the user's own words when you reflect them back.
- Do not interrogate. When want, obstacle, stakes and change exist, stop and build.
